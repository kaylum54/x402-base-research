# Tracing Real Money Through Base x402

## 1. Bottom line

x402 on Base is a working rail with little commerce on it. The protocol now has a Linux Foundation home and 58 member organisations ([x402.org](https://x402.org/members/)), Coinbase's facilitator settles **91% of Base transactions**, AWS made x402 payments generally available on 2026-08-18, and Cloudflare opened a US-only closed beta of the seller side on 2026-09-30. The money is small. In the six months to 2026-10-11 x402scan shows **41.5M Base transactions and $5.66M of USDC**, and monthly dollars fell from $1.93M in April to $0.57M in September ([x402scan](https://www.x402scan.com/api/trpc/networks.bucketedStatistics?input=%7B%22json%22%3A%7B%22timeframe%22%3A0%2C%22numBuckets%22%3A400%7D%7D)). About **27% of those dollars, and 49% at the most, look like API purchases** once recipients are sorted by hand. One facilitator, Meridian, carried 36% of the dollars, and 41 wallets paying each other make up 96% of its lifetime volume. A single BlockRun wallet made 64% of all Base transactions.

The builders to follow daily are BlockRun, PayAI, Olas, Bankr, Virtuals and Merit Systems, the team behind x402scan. Venice, Crossmint and thirdweb are serious companies that treat x402 as a side door. The trend-hoppers cluster around tokens. AX1, Cluster Protocol, Meridian, Questflow and Heurist each score 4 of 5, and 24 of the 63 token-wave profiles score 4 or 5. The October 2025 mint copycats (PING and six look-alikes) took in about $603K of USDC, and admin accounts withdrew 75% of it. ATXP ($19.2M seed), AnySpend, Questflow, Relai, Mogami and x402.rs have stopped or slowed to near zero on x402, and elizaOS's token was declared dead on 2026-08-05.

Solana out-counted Base on 19 of 184 days and carried 76% of x402 transactions in the four weeks to 2026-09-22 on Artemis data ([Solana Compass](https://solanacompass.com/news/solana-processes-76-of-all-x402-ai-agent-transactions-232-million-in-four-weeks)). On x402scan Base still had 81% of transactions and 66% of dollars over the last 30 days, and the August swing looks like one BlockRun payer changing chain (the researchers' inference). An April analysis of adjusted volume found about 4,000 wallets drove about 90% of real spending, with Base carrying the bulk of it ([The Defiant](https://thedefiant.io/news/infrastructure/visa-s-sheffield-pegs-adjusted-x402-volume-at-19m)). Watch BlockRun's releases and chain split, the CDP changelog and facilitator price, the Cloudflare Monetization Gateway docs for a fee or volume figures, Stripe's network table (MPP on Tempo and Solana, x402 on Base), and three token events: the $AX1 launch, dTelecom's DTEL token event and Cluster payments restarting. Cloudflare's Monetization Gateway is a closed beta that lets sellers charge agents. It is US-only, and the agent wallet is a separate product that cannot hold funds yet.

## 2. Coinbase and Cloudflare have shipped real rails, and Cloudflare's Monetization Gateway is the seller side

The protocol is still "Version 2". It ships as SDK minor releases, from `@x402/core` v2.0.0 on 2025-12-11 to v2.28.0 on 2026-09-29, with about 100 commits a month in the repository ([tags](https://github.com/x402-foundation/x402/tags)).

Coinbase's own scale claims use different scopes. The Q1 deck said more than 100M total x402 payments had been processed as of Q1 2026 ([Q1 deck](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1679788/000167978826000053/q126earningsdeck-finalse.htm)). The Q2 deck said over 97% of onchain agentic transactions used x402 and over 90% of agentic stablecoin volume sat on Base, found by counting programmatic wallet signatures and HTTP 402 interactions ([Q2 deck](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1679788/000167978826000087/q226earningsdeck_sec.htm)). A share measured by counting 402 interactions is close to true by definition. Base's own post for the 30 days to 2026-05-29 gave 3.1M transactions and $1.2M, about $0.39 a payment, which is small beside the $17T of stablecoin volume Base cited for 2025 ([Base feed](https://api.paragraph.com/blogs/rss/@base)).

The Foundation is neutral on paper. A three-seat Technical Steering Committee (Coinbase, Cloudflare, Stripe) decides by majority, and a 75% supermajority is needed to change the core principles, remove a member or leave Apache-2.0 ([charter](https://github.com/x402-foundation/x402/blob/main/foundation/x402%20Technical%20Charter%20March%2031%2C%202026.pdf)). The members page on 2026-10-11 listed 58 organisations, 19 of them Premier at $200,000 a year, and a Premier member is expected to commit substantial engineering, for example 2.0 FTE or more ([members](https://x402.org/members/), [agreement](https://cdn.platform.linuxfoundation.org/agreements/x402-foundation.pdf)). Coinbase still holds the first TSC seat and the top committer slots. No board names, chair, treasurer or official X account have been published, and the roadmap file still promises an update. Base's own agent push is lighter: base.org/agents lists x402, ERC-8004, Agentic Wallets and Base MCP, and Base Batches 004 has no x402 track ([Base Batches](https://www.base.org/batches)).

Cloudflare's dated record shows one live x402 client, one closed-beta seller product and a wallet that exists only as a handle.

The Gateway works like this. The seller sets rules, a price and a wallet. The agent gets a 402, signs a payment authorisation and retries. The Gateway verifies it, settles through Coinbase's facilitator in USDC on Base using x402 v2 (`exact` for fixed prices, `upto` for variable ones) and returns the resource ([beta post](https://blog.cloudflare.com/monetization-gateway-beta/)). The docs give a minimum price of $0.001 and a maximum of $100 a request, and publish no Cloudflare fee. The beta post names Cloudflare AI Gateway, Ceramic.ai, Stocktwits and API2PDF as launch users, with no volumes ([beta post](https://blog.cloudflare.com/monetization-gateway-beta/)). An earlier draft quoted a Cloudflare executive on daily 402 volume; that figure is not in the cited call transcript and has been removed (correction, 2026-10-14). The CFO's description of Wallets (2026-09-09) comes from a third-party transcript, and the Wallets docs of 2026-08-19 still showed handle reservation only ([Wallets docs](https://developers.cloudflare.com/wallets/)).

> **Monetization Gateway at a glance**
>
> **What it is:** Cloudflare's seller-side product. A site, API or MCP tool sets a price and AI agents pay it per request over x402. Closed beta from 2026-09-30, after a waitlist announced on 2026-07-01 ([docs hub](https://developers.cloudflare.com/monetization-gateway/)).
>
> **What is easy to miss:** buyers and sellers must both be in the US, so UK sites cannot join yet. Sellers need a card on file, a verified email, an account older than 60 days and a proxied zone older than 30 days. Payment settles in USDC on Base through Coinbase's facilitator. The documented minimum is $0.001 a request, and no Cloudflare fee is published. The wallet that would let an agent hold money and pay on its own is a separate product, Cloudflare Wallets, which cannot hold funds yet, and an agent only pays once its owner funds it.

Among other large companies, AWS has shipped the most. Bedrock AgentCore Payments (x402 plus MPP, Coinbase and Stripe/Privy wallets) went generally available on 2026-08-18 ([AWS](https://aws.amazon.com/blogs/machine-learning/amazon-bedrock-agentcore-payments-is-now-generally-available-enabling-agents-to-transact-safely-and-autonomously-at-scale/)). Circle's x402-compatible Nanopayments reached mainnet on 2026-04-29, with a $0.000001 minimum ([Circle](https://www.circle.com/blog/nanopayments-powered-by-circle-gateway-is-now-live-on-mainnet)). Stripe takes x402 payments on Base for US businesses outside New York, with a private preview in the EU and a few other regions but not the UK, and it also promotes its own MPP ([Stripe docs](https://docs.stripe.com/payments/machine)). Visa and Mastercard have no first-party x402 product, and OpenAI appears only in a testnet cookbook example ([Visa](https://www.visa.com/en-us/thought-leadership/innovation/agentic-payments-from-the-ground-up)). None of the seven company quotes in the April Linux Foundation release claimed a shipped x402 product.

## 3. Read x402 numbers as automation first, because about 27% of Base dollars look like commerce

x402scan is the only free source with daily series by chain, facilitator and recipient, so nearly every figure in this report comes from it, with the Coinbase Bazaar and x402-list.com as cross-checks. It counts indexed USDC transfers sent by facilitators in its registry. It does not count HTTP calls, it cannot tell a paid API call from a payment between related wallets, it covers only Base, Solana, Polygon and Optimism, and it hides facilitators under 100 transactions in a window. Its "unique buyers" are summed per facilitator, so a wallet that uses two facilitators counts twice ([x402scan source](https://github.com/Merit-Systems/x402scan)).

The researchers who gathered the underlying data sorted every large Base recipient for 2026-04-11 to 2026-10-11 into classes.

| Class of Base recipient | Recipients | Transactions | USDC | Share of USDC |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Meridian round-trip wallets | 19 | 79K | $2.04M | 36.1% |
| Registered multi-buyer sellers (average ticket under $500) | 95 | 36.5M | $1.36M | 24.0% |
| High-ticket one-offs | 7 | 438 | $486K | 8.6% |
| Few-party large transfers (FluxA flows) | 12 | 91 | $447K | 7.9% |
| One other few-party transfer | 1 | 8 | $407K | 7.2% |
| Unregistered multi-buyer sellers | 58 | 2.48M | $190K | 3.4% |
| Long tail, not itemised | about 38.8K active in 30 days alone | 2.41M | $733K | 13.0% |
| **Base total** | | **41.5M** | **$5.66M** | 100% |

Only the two multi-buyer rows resemble API commerce: $1.55M, which is **27% of Base dollars and 94% of transactions**. Adding the unsorted tail and the one-offs gives an upper bound of $2.77M, or 49%. That is $8.4K to $15K a day of API-style spending against $30.8K a day reported in total. CoinDesk's March 2026 estimate of about $28K a day of real commerce (via presenc.ai) has the same order of magnitude, so the level has fallen since then.

Transaction counts mostly record automation. Five gateways (BlockRun, claw402, Vishwa, AX1 and AIsa) supplied **79% of the window's Base transactions and 9% of its dollars**, and each headline traces to one mechanism. One BlockRun wallet made 26.7M transactions ([x402scan](https://www.x402scan.com/recipient/0xe9030014f5dae217d0a152f02a043567b16c1abf)). Claw402's 4.87M transactions are 25 to 40 wallets polling $0.002 endpoints all day. Vishwa is a fixed fleet of about 300 wallets paying $0.001. AX1's $0.02 route is the entry fee to a weekly rewards contest, with call peaks every Sunday before the Monday cutoff, as AX1 explained in its own post ([AX1 Telegram](https://t.me/ax1vc/3166?embed=1&mode=tme)). About half of AIsa's 31.5M transactions plausibly came from a cohort of about 40 similar wallets (five were checked) that stopped within minutes of each other on 2025-12-31 ([x402scan](https://www.x402scan.com/recipient/0x66fa4d79ca84016b42352be33c908dd812952ec8)). The same pattern appears below the top. About 94% of FluxA's 30-day transactions are $0.001 payments from some 1,300 wallets that fire on timers ([FluxA on x402scan](https://www.x402scan.com/facilitator/fluxa)). AnySpend's recorded volume is 99.9% B3's own ad marketplace, paid by about 260 wallets. Twenty-nine wallets supply 98.7% of dTelecom's dollars. Cluster Protocol's token disclosure counted x402 payers as airdrop-eligible ([Cluster disclosure](https://hub.clusterprotocol.ai/public-disclosure)).

The 2025 history explains why sources differ. Base logged 111.3M transactions and $35.1M of USDC in October to December 2025, five recipients took 90.7% of November's dollars, and PING, the first pay-to-mint token, received 97.2% of all Base transactions on 2025-10-22. Chainalysis credits the Q4 jump largely to meme-coin activity, especially PING, and found payer retention of 87% during the rush and 5% after it ([Chainalysis](https://www.chainalysis.com/blog/x402-agentic-payments-adoption/)). Verified mint contracts are only 0.24% of all-time Base transactions, so November's 64.3M transactions came mostly from a few payees receiving millions of small payments.

Independent estimates agree with the hand sorting. Artemis marked an average of 63.94% of transactions as gamed from 2025-11-09 to 2026-02-07 ([4pillars](https://research.4pillars.io/en/research/x402-on-polygon-real-or-gamed)). Visa and Artemis put wash-filtered volume at about $15.0M over 109.6M transactions since May 2025, as of 2026-04-21, with roughly 4,000 wallets (the top 1% of buyers) behind about 90% of it ([Visa](https://www.visa.com/en-us/thought-leadership/innovation/agentic-payments-from-the-ground-up)). In the last 30 days one FluxA wallet paid 38.4% of Base dollars in 18 transactions, and the 25 largest recipients held 89.9% of $703K. The Bazaar lists 32,169 resources, the median one drew 2 calls in 30 days, and AX1 alone holds 43.8% of all calls ([Bazaar API](https://api.cdp.coinbase.com/platform/v2/x402/discovery/resources?limit=1000&offset=0)).

Three published figures do not reconcile and should stay out of charts. The x402.org home page shows 75.41M transactions and $24.24M for 30 days, but it is stamped 2026-08-25 and runs 5.2 times x402scan on transactions and 19 times on dollars ([x402.org](https://x402.org)). Valoria reports 2.7 times the whole market's volume. The x402watch wash filter flagged $130,774 as wash in its 2026-08-10 snapshot, $57 on 2026-09-10 and $979 on 2026-10-10, while the Meridian loop alone moved $129K in the last 30 days ([x402watch data](https://github.com/printmoneylab/x402watch-data)). x402-list.com matches x402scan's 30-day facilitator dollars (Coinbase $572K against $580K, FluxA $322K on both, Meridian $131K against $129K), so use the pair together ([x402-list.com](https://x402-list.com)).

Rank projects by distinct buyers over 30 days, dollars per buyer and repeat buyers across weeks, and treat transaction rank as an index of automation. On-chain counts will also fall without demand falling, because batching is spreading: Solana batch settlement began on 2026-10-07, Circle's Gateway batches x402 payments, and x402's `batch-settlement` scheme is live on EVM. Developer interest is the one measure that is clearly rising. `@x402/core` downloads grew from 11,586 a day in April to 41,913 in September (3.6 times) and the PyPI package from 3,720 to 6,489 a day, though both counts include CI runs and transitive installs ([npm](https://api.npmjs.org/downloads/range/2026-04-01:2026-10-10/x402-fetch), [pypistats](https://pypistats.org/api/packages/x402/overall?mirrors=false)).

## 6. Trend-hoppers follow one arc, a hot narrative, then a token, then silence

Questflow changed its headline about every six months (AI workflows, DeFAI, the agent economy and x402, prediction markets, a finance agent) and dropped its x402 facilitator about three months after launch. Capminal ran five narratives in 17 months. Cluster Protocol launched its token in the same quarter as its x402 gateway. Copycats move within days: after PING's mint on 2025-10-22, x420 issued a token on 10-23, Heurist opened a vending-machine repo on 10-24 and the x402.team tokens appeared on 10-25.

## 7. Solana wins the transaction count, Stripe's MPP threatens the default, and facilitator pricing squeezes everyone

Solana passed Base on daily x402 transactions on 2026-08-25 and carried 76% of x402 transactions (23.2M) in the four weeks to 2026-09-22 on Artemis data ([Solana Compass](https://solanacompass.com/news/solana-flips-base-in-daily-x402-transactions-for-the-first-time-in-six-months)). x402scan agrees on direction: Solana led on 19 of 184 days, and Base's weekly share of the two chains' transactions fell to 5.9% in the week of 2026-08-24 and was back to 76.8% by the week of 2026-09-14. The same data has Solana ahead on 26 of 28 days from 2026-01-22 to 2026-02-18, so the "first time in six months" framing does not hold there. Named causes are BlockRun's routing, Ramp's agent-wallet alpha on Solana (2026-08-20 or 21), AWS CloudFront's pay-per-crawler integration and Coinbase's own Solana facilitator upgrade on 2026-09-18 ([Solana Compass](https://solanacompass.com/news/solana-processes-76-of-all-x402-ai-agent-transactions-232-million-in-four-weeks)). Wallet-level data suggests the BlockRun part was a single payer changing chain. A lead built on a few routing decisions can reverse, and Solana's share has swung between 49.7% and 88% this year ([Crypto Briefing](https://cryptobriefing.com/solana-x402-market-share-dominance/)). On dollars Base still leads, with 66% over the last 30 days and about 93% in the last wash-filtered split.

Stripe and Tempo's Machine Payments Protocol is the protocol-level rival. It launched with Tempo mainnet on 2026-03-18, and Cloudflare's docs call it backward-compatible with x402. Stripe's docs list MPP on Tempo and Solana and x402 on Base only, with a $0.50 card minimum and $0.01 for stablecoin payments ([Stripe](https://docs.stripe.com/payments/machine)). Measured MPP usage is small, about $25,000 over 115,000 transactions as of 2026-04-21 ([Visa](https://www.visa.com/en-us/thought-leadership/innovation/agentic-payments-from-the-ground-up)), and Tempo's $1B of rolling 30-day transfers on 2026-09-02 was mostly enterprise payouts ([Crypto Briefing](https://cryptobriefing.com/tempo-1b-stablecoin-transfer-volume/)). The threat is distribution. A Stripe merchant who wants stablecoins and does not care about the protocol is steered to MPP on non-Base rails, and MPP has sessions built in while x402's `batch-settlement` is EVM-only and newer ([QuickNode](https://www.quicknode.com/blog/x402-vs-mpp)).

Facilitator pricing is the second squeeze. CDP charges $0.001 after 1,000 free transactions a month, while Dexter, Mogami and Polygon's own facilitator are free and thirdweb takes 0.3%, and Binance's B402 sponsors gas and copies CDP's v2 spec so that clients change only a URL ([Binance docs](https://developers.binance.com/en/docs/products/onchainpay-x402/introduction)). A seller pricing calls at $0.001 or less cannot cover a CDP fee without batching, which is why the 2026 work is on `upto` and batch settlement, and why Circle's Nanopayments (mainnet 2026-04-29, 65,480 downloads of `@circle-fin/x402-batching` in 30 days) and Arc (mainnet 2026-09-16) matter. Gateways are already drifting away from per-call x402: BlockRun added card-funded keys, AIsa moved to API keys and batched settlement, and Locus sells a prepaid balance. The researchers' likely end state is a 402 layer where a server lists several payment methods and the client picks one, with durable value in discovery, identity and spend controls, and billing. Base-only facilitators and wallets are the most exposed.

## 8. A daily watch costs about $35 a month and fits inside one Cloudflare Worker

### 8.1 The Worker collects, a Claude Code routine judges, Discord receives

Many x402 watchers still follow the releases feed of `coinbase/x402`, whose newest item is dated 2026-04-13. The repository moved to `x402-foundation/x402` (newest release 2026-09-29), so point feeds there and add that repository's commits feed (newest 2026-10-10).

The watch runs as job kinds inside one Cloudflare Worker with a Durable Object job queue, cron triggers, D1, a bearer-protected API for the digest routine and a Discord webhook. The Worker collects and scores with fixed rules and makes no LLM API calls. The daily judgement runs as a scheduled Claude Code routine.

```
06:15 UTC  Worker collects: GitHub (Atom, REST with ETag), npm and PyPI, Bazaar, feeds, X, x402scan -> D1 watch_* tables
06:45 UTC  Worker scores: type x entity x novelty x recency, flags, tier-1 alerts -> #x402-alerts (rules only, no model)
07:30 UTC  Claude Code routine (scheduled): GET /api/watch/context -> judgement, web search on flagged items only
           -> POST /api/watch/digest
07:30+     Worker stores the digest, posts it to #x402-watch, dedupes by content hash
Sunday     Health-review routine: the stall list
```

The store is six D1 `watch_*` tables (entities, handles, events, metrics, flags, digests). The new job kinds are `watch.github`, `watch.registry`, `watch.bazaar`, `watch.feeds`, `watch.x` and `watch.score`. The endpoints are `POST /x/webhook` for X push events and the two routine endpoints behind a bearer token. Nothing has been built yet and the design is unprototyped, so D1 volume, Durable Object time and the Bazaar crawl duration are estimates. The research probes ran from a desktop machine, and GeckoTerminal returned 429 to 56 of 56 calls from Cloudflare's shared IPs, so test each collector from a Worker preview.

Everything is collected daily, and tiers decide who can trigger an alert and who gets a line in the digest.

### 8.3 Use a hybrid X layer at about $19 a month

The official X API is pay-per-use for new sign-ups: $0.005 per post read, $0.010 per user, and at most 3M post reads a month ([X pricing](https://docs.x.com/x-api/getting-started/pricing)). Reading about 300 accounts costs $45 a month at 300 posts a day, $90 at 600 and $270 at 1,800. Two resellers charge roughly 25 to 33 times less, twitterapi.io at $0.15 per 1,000 tweets and SocialData at $0.0002 an item ([twitterapi.io](https://twitterapi.io/pricing), [SocialData](https://socialdata.tools/pricing.md)). Neither publishes uptime data, and X sent cease-and-desist letters over Nitter on 2026-08-24, so vendor continuity is the main risk ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/08/25/x-sends-cease-and-desist-to-open-source-project-nitter-over-alleged-scraping/)).

The recommendation is a hybrid. X's Activity API pushes events for about 50 core accounts (new posts and nine kinds of profile change) for $15 to $30 a month ([X Activity API](https://docs.x.com/x-api/activity/introduction)). The other 250 accounts go through SocialData query monitors or twitterapi.io time-filtered search for $3 to $8, and twitterapi.io batch profile snapshots cost cents a day. Keep the adapter thin and store post IDs and URLs only. Each week compare five accounts against the official API as a canary. If a vendor fails, point the long tail at the official API behind the same adapter. Do not poll timelines on a per-item provider: it bills every tweet returned, so 300 accounts polled once a day cost about $36 a month at SocialData rates. Use `from:a OR from:b since_time:` searches or monitors that bill only new tweets, about $2 a month for 15 queries. Use xAI's `x_search` ($5 per 1,000 posts plus tokens) for one-off questions only.

X's developer guidelines bar monitoring users without consent, require express opt-in for matching X accounts to off-X identities, and forbid scraping ([guidelines](https://docs.x.com/developer-guidelines)). The plan therefore watches project accounts and publicly identified team members' professional accounts only, stores IDs, URLs and derived signals instead of post bodies, honours deletions within 24 hours, never infers sensitive attributes, and posts links and one-line summaries to Discord instead of verbatim text. For named people in the UK, check UK GDPR before storing the GitHub-to-X mapping.

### 8.4 Find engineers' X accounts cheapest-first and score each match

Seed names from team pages, public GitHub org members, top contributors and npm and PyPI maintainers. The GitHub `twitter_username` field is free and was filled in for 23 of the first 45 human contributors to the x402 repository (51%), so it is the first pass ([GitHub users API](https://docs.github.com/en/rest/users/users)). Next, Neynar's `by_x_username` lookup returns Farcaster users who proved they own an X account, which is strong evidence ([Neynar](https://docs.neynar.com/reference/lookup-users-by-x-username)). ENS text records (`com.twitter`) add self-declared links ([ENSIP-5](https://docs.ens.domains/ensip/5)), and team pages and personal sites usually link the handle outright. DataForSEO is for the leftovers: a `site:x.com "Full Name" x402` query costs $0.003 in the Standard queue or $0.01 Live, so 300 names cost $0.90 to $3 ([pricing](https://dataforseo.com/pricing/serp/google-organic-serp-api)). Confirm the `cost` field on the first response, because the multiplier for operators is ambiguous. Finally, fetch each candidate's profile through X's `users/by` call at $0.01 a user, about $3 for 300, and read the bio, URL and affiliation.

Score every match. Add 3 for a Farcaster-verified link, 2 for a reciprocal link from the X profile to the person's GitHub or site, 2 for an ENS record, 2 for a team page link, 1 for the GitHub field alone, 1 if the bio names the project, 1 for an affiliation badge, and 0.5 each for a fuzzy name match and a search-only hit. Accept at 3 or more, send 1.5 to 3 to a review queue and reject below 1.5. A self-declared GitHub field can be stale and impersonators copy names, so reciprocity counts most. The one-off pass for 300 people costs $5 to $25 and about a fifth need a manual review. Re-verify monthly with ETag calls and a daily profile diff, and flag any handle whose bio stops naming the project. Record the evidence for each match and do not publish the mapping.

### 8.5 Rank events by type, then raise flags for stalling, trend-hopping and wash volume

Score each event as type weight times entity weight times novelty times recency. Entity weight is 3 for core, 2 for tracked and 1 for candidates. Example type weights are 10 for a major release, 9 for a maintainer change, an archived or transferred repository, a core person's bio change or a linked token launch, 6 for a release train, a new repository by a tracked person or a 30% fall in weekly Bazaar payers, and 1 for a routine post. Collapse repeats by entity, day and type, cap each project at three lines, and demote hiring and policy items. The routine reads the top 40 events and the flags, searches the web only where a flag needs context, and writes the digest in short plain sentences. The weights are proposals, untested on x402 data, so run the collectors for two to four weeks and then tune them.

| Flag | Rule (proposal) |
|---|---|
| Commit decay | Four-week mean of weekly commits below 40% of the prior 12-week mean for two weeks, or no default-branch commit in 21 days |
| Release stall | Time since the last release above three times the median of the previous six gaps |
| Concentration | One contributor accounts for half of all contributions, or the top two have zero commits in four weeks ([CHAOSS](https://chaoss.community/kb/metric-contributor-absence-factor/)) |
| Team departure | Affiliate badge removed, bio no longer names the project, handle or display name changed, or a core contributor silent 30 days after a bio change |
| Usage fade | Bazaar last-called time older than 7 days on a steady service, or three weekly declines in unique payers or downloads |
| Trend-hopping | Two or more new narrative terms in bios, taglines or repo descriptions within 14 days, strongest with commit decay or a first token mention |
| New token | A contract address or a ticker next to "CA" in post text, or a deploy by a wallet linked to the project |
| Wash or incentive volume | One wallet above 50% of a seller's 30-day transactions; 80% or more of payments at one price; payers firing at fixed intervals; payers that only ever paid this seller; volume that stops on one date |

The last row comes from the cases in this report: BlockRun's single wallet, FluxA's timed fleet, AnySpend's 260 wallets, AX1's contest and the 2025-12-31 stop at AIsa. The commit and release rules should be measured against the benchmark in section 5, about 20 commits a week and a release every month or so, rather than against absolute counts.
