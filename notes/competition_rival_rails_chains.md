# Competition around x402 on Base: rival agent-payment rails and competing chains (notes as of 2026-10-11)

Scope: rivalry only. Coinbase, Base, Cloudflare and the x402 work of Google, Stripe, Visa, Circle, AWS and Vercel are covered by other researchers, so they appear here only where they are the rival or the context. Window: 2026-04-11 to 2026-10-11. No token prices or charts. Source labels: primary means the project's or company's own page; secondary means news or analysis; vendor claim means a company describing its own product; single-source and unconfirmed are flagged where they apply. My web-search budget ran out part-way through the work (the tool reported its per-turn limit), so later checks were done by direct page fetch only. Limits are listed under each Gaps heading.

Short verdict (details and caveats below):
- On transaction counts, Base has lost the lead to Solana. Solana passed Base on daily count on 2026-08-25 and carried 76% of x402 transactions in the four weeks to 2026-09-22 (Artemis data).
- On wash-filtered dollars, the last public split (Visa/Artemis, data to 2026-04-21) had Base at about 93% of adjusted volume, and Coinbase's Q2 results, as reported on 2026-07-30, put more than 90% of agentic stablecoin volume on Base (the report cites no filing). Nobody has published a wash-filtered split since April, so the claim that Base is losing ground is shown for counts and unproven for dollars.
- The rival that matters most at the protocol level is Stripe and Tempo's Machine Payments Protocol (MPP). It is backwards-compatible with x402, Stripe's docs steer merchants to it first, and Stripe's x402 support is on Base only while its MPP stablecoin support is on Tempo and Solana. Its measured agent usage is still small.
- Visa, Mastercard and Google are hedging by sitting in several camps (x402 Foundation, MPP, AP2, UCP); PayPal backs ACP, UCP and AP2 and shows no x402 link. None of their own agent-payment products has published much usage data.

## Q1. What share of x402 activity has each chain had over the last six months and why; which Solana-first projects are taking share from Base; where do Kite AI, BNB Chain projects (Pieverse and others), Circle's Arc and Tempo stand?

### Takeaway
Solana now leads x402 by transaction count (flip on 2026-08-25; 76% of transactions in the four weeks to 2026-09-22), but the last wash-filtered split (2026-04-21) had Base at about 93% of adjusted volume, so Base is losing the count race and has not been shown to be losing the dollar race. Polygon had a strong second half of January (62% of real transactions) and was third in the April adjusted ranking; BNB Chain, Avalanche, Sei and Kite have docs or launches but no visible x402 volume; Tempo and Arc are real chains with agent-payment features whose agent-specific usage is thin.

### Inferences
- Two scoreboards, not one. By transaction count (x402scan, x402.org, Artemis raw feeds), Base lost the lead on 2026-08-25 and trails heavily by September. By wash-filtered dollars, Base led as of 2026-04-21 and Coinbase claims a similar picture for Q2. The data to say whether Base's dollar lead survived Q3 is not public.
- Average ticket size shows why counts mislead (my arithmetic from the cited figures): Solana cumulative is about $0.29 per transaction ($10M over 35M); the stale x402.org 30-day figure is about $0.32; the Visa/Artemis adjusted figure is about $0.14 ($15.0M over 109.6M). A chain that wins on count can still be small in dollars.
- The four-week Artemis total implied by 23.2M being 76% is about 30.5M transactions, far below the x402.org 30-day figure of 75.41M. The two cannot both be total x402 activity; they differ in filter, window or freshness. Do not mix them in one chart.
- Solana's lead looks like a few routing decisions (a gateway such as BlockRun, an enterprise alpha such as Ramp, a distributor such as CloudFront) rather than broad facilitator adoption. Share on this kind of lead is reversible: Solana's share swung between 49.7% and 88% earlier in 2026, and Polygon had a 62% real-transaction fortnight in January.
- Facilitator and wallet availability follows share. Polygon's rise coincided with Polygon's own facilitator and, later, CDP support; Solana's runs through Dexter, Corbits, PayAI and Coinbase itself. A chain without a free or sponsored facilitator and a wallet path is unlikely to gain share.
- Real strategic rivals among chains are those with a distribution wedge: Solana (gateways and enterprise on-ramps), Tempo (Stripe's default chain and the MPP home), BNB Chain (Binance wallet distribution and agent-identity lead, though x402 is still testnet or by request), Arc (USDC-native chain plus Circle's batching). Avalanche, Sei, Kite and Pieverse show no volume evidence.
- Kite's claim to be a hub for x402, AP2, MPP and MCP reads as a wallet and identity play rather than a share-taker on x402 volume.

## Q2. For each rival protocol or rail, what has shipped, how much adoption is visible, and does it use, extend or bypass x402?

### Takeaway
MPP is the only rival that attacks x402 at the protocol layer while staying compatible with it, and it has Stripe's distribution behind it, but visible agent usage is small (about $25,000 over about 115,000 transactions in its first weeks). The card-network and Google protocols (ACP, AP2, UCP, Visa TAP and Intelligent Commerce, Mastercard Agent Pay and AP4M) mostly handle identity, authorisation and consumer checkout and either extend x402 (AP2 has an x402 extension) or sit beside it; none has published agent-payment volume. L402, Skyfire, Catena, Nevermined and TollBit are small or enablers, and TollBit does not use x402.

### Inferences
- MPP's threat is strategic, not yet volumetric. It reaches Stripe merchants (card plus stablecoin, with Stripe's tax, fraud and refund tools), has an IETF path, supports many rails, and can serve x402 clients from the same endpoint. Its measured agent usage is small, and Tempo's large numbers are payout flows. Stripe's own docs route x402 to Base only and MPP to Tempo and Solana, so a Stripe merchant who wants stablecoins and does not care about the protocol is steered toward MPP on non-Base rails.
- The convergence at the wire level (HTTP 402 plus a signed credential) means rivals mostly compete for who owns wallet, identity, discovery and billing, not for the 402 header itself.
- The card-network and Google stack is aimed at consumer checkout and authorisation. It is more a complement than a displacement for machine micropayments today, and it hedges by sitting inside the x402 Foundation. The risk to x402 projects is that agentic checkout defaults to cards for anything above micropayment size (Stripe's card floor is $0.50).
- OpenAI's retreat from in-chat checkout, plus Stripe's pivot to UCP for Google surfaces, shows consumer agent checkout is fragmenting faster than machine payments. That tends to push investor and press attention toward whichever protocol has live surfaces (UCP/Gemini).
- Skyfire, TollBit, L402 and Catena are unlikely to take volume from Base x402 projects in the next six months; Nevermined and OKX are more likely to ride on x402 than replace it.

## Q3. Dynamics: card networks vs stablecoins; standardising on x402 or fragmenting; facilitator commoditisation; specific risks to projects that run only on Base

### Takeaway
Everyone big is hedging: Visa, Mastercard, Stripe, Google, Cloudflare and Shopify sit on the x402 Foundation board while also backing or building MPP, AP2, UCP, TAP or AP4M in some form, so the likely outcome is multi-protocol endpoints rather than one winner. Facilitators are commoditising (free options, chain-run options, and Coinbase's CDP at $0.001 per transaction after 1,000 free a month), which leaves Base-only projects exposed on narrative, on facilitator pricing power and on concentration in a few buyers.

### Cited Findings

#### Card networks vs stablecoins
- Visa's stablecoin settlement run rate was about $7B as of March 2026 and it lists 160+ stablecoin-linked card programs; its agent products run on tokenised card credentials — [Visa release, 2026-06-10](https://investor.visa.com/news/news-details/2026/Visa-Announces-New-AI-Stablecoin-and-Token-Innovations-to-Power-Intelligent-Programmable-Commerce-at-Visa-Payments-Forum/default.aspx).
- Visa and Artemis conclude the outcome will not come down to cards versus stablecoins, and that some tasks will use both — [Visa](https://www.visa.com/en-us/thought-leadership/innovation/agentic-payments-from-the-ground-up).
- Mastercard bought into stablecoins (BVNK, up to $1.8B) and built AP4M across cards, accounts and stablecoins; its CEO tied agentic commerce and stablecoins together on the Q2 call — [Fortune](https://fortune.com/2026/03/17/mastercard-bvnk-acquisition-stablecoins-1-8-billion); [DigitalTransactions](https://www.digitaltransactions.net/mastercard-focuses-on-gains-in-crypto-and-agentic-commerce/).
- Stripe's docs put a $0.50 minimum on card (SPT) agent payments and $0.01 USDC on stablecoin payments, with MPP sessions able to bill sub-cent increments and settle at $0.01 USDC minimum — [Stripe docs](https://docs.stripe.com/payments/machine).

#### Standardising on x402 or fragmenting
- Linux Foundation announced the x402 Foundation on 2026-04-02 with 22 organisations expressing intent (including Adyen, AWS, American Express, Base, Circle, Cloudflare, Coinbase, Fiserv, Google, Mastercard, Microsoft, Polygon Labs, Shopify, Solana Foundation, Stripe, thirdweb and Visa); the protocol was initially developed by Coinbase, Cloudflare and Stripe; the announcement names no rival protocol — [Linux Foundation](https://www.linuxfoundation.org/press/linux-foundation-is-launching-the-x402-foundation-and-welcoming-the-contribution-of-the-x402-protocol).
- Operational launch 2026-07-14: 40 founding organisations; 17 premier members hold governing-board seats (Adyen, AWS, American Express, Circle, Cloudflare, Coinbase, Fiserv, Google, Mastercard, Monad Foundation, MoonPay, Ripple, Shopify, Solana Foundation, Stellar Development Foundation, Stripe, Visa); general members include Fireblocks, Injective, LayerZero Labs, NEAR Foundation, Polygon Labs and Quant Network; Base is not named as a member in that article — [Solana Compass, 2026-07-15](https://solanacompass.com/news/linux-foundation-launches-x402-foundation-as-open-standards-body-for-ai-agent-payments). PayPal and OpenAI are not in the lists I retrieved.
- The x402 repo now lives under the x402-foundation organisation (6.7k stars, 2.2k forks), states the standard will not force reliance on a single party, and advises mainnet operators not to assume the public x402.org facilitator is their default; the coinbase/x402 repo is described as a development fork — [x402-foundation/x402](https://github.com/x402-foundation/x402); [coinbase/x402](https://github.com/coinbase/x402).
- The same companies back rival or adjacent stacks: Stripe co-authors MPP and ACP; Visa contributes to MPP and runs TAP; Mastercard is an MPP design partner and runs AP4M; Google runs AP2 and UCP; Mastercard donates Verifiable Intent to FIDO — see Q2. Stripe lists both MPP and x402 in its docs — [Stripe docs](https://docs.stripe.com/payments/machine).
- Multi-protocol support is common: mppx emits both x402 and MPP challenges from one endpoint — [mpp.dev: MPP vs x402](https://mpp.dev/mpp-vs-x402); Faremeter supports x402 v1/v2 and MPP on Solana — [Faremeter](https://github.com/faremeter/faremeter); Kite markets itself as a hub for x402, AP2, MPP and MCP — [The Block (press release)](https://www.theblock.co/post/399534/kite-launches-kite-chain-and-kite-agent-passport-enabling-autonomous-ai-agent-payments); Nevermined lists x402 and MPP — [Nevermined](https://nevermined.ai); OKX APP builds on both — [OKX docs](https://web3.okx.com/onchainos/dev-docs/payments/overview).
- GitHub attention (stars on 2026-10-11; a rough proxy): x402-foundation/x402 6.7k; UCP 3.4k; AP2 3.2k; ACP 1.6k; Tempo node about 1.0k; a2a-x402 564; Visa TAP 212; mpp-rs 91; Faremeter 69 — see the repo links in Q2 and Q1.

#### Facilitator commoditisation
- Coinbase's CDP facilitator: first 1,000 onchain transactions a month free, then $0.001 each; verification free; networks Base, Polygon, Arbitrum, World and Solana (Solana `exact` and `upto`; EVM also `batch-settlement`) — [CDP network support](https://docs.cdp.coinbase.com/x402/network-support); [CDP facilitator docs](https://docs.cdp.coinbase.com/x402/core-concepts/facilitator).
- Alternatives listed in the x402 docs: Dexter (free, no account), Built on Stellar (free), Mogami (Base, free), Polygon's own, NEAR (NEAR and Base, sponsored gas), PayAI (no API keys), Corbits (EVM and Solana), Celo (gasless), thirdweb (listed in Polygon and Avalanche docs), Fireblocks, XRPL, Canton and others — [x402 docs](https://docs.x402.org/dev-tools/facilitators). Binance's B402 sponsors gas and matches the CDP v2 spec field for field, so CDP-built clients work with a URL change — [Binance docs](https://developers.binance.com/en/docs/products/onchainpay-x402/introduction).
- Share history of facilitators (January 2026): Coinbase had 60 to 70% of daily transactions in October to early November 2025, then fell to a quarter to a third once Dexter passed it — [Tangem](https://tangem.com/en/news/adoption/8246-dexter-overtakes-coinbase-as-top-x402-transaction-facilitator/) (search-index excerpt). No later facilitator-share data found.
- Coinbase now runs a Solana facilitator and upgraded it on 2026-09-18 (`upto` support, 66% lower verification latency) — [Solana Compass, 2026-09-22](https://solanacompass.com/news/solana-processes-76-of-all-x402-ai-agent-transactions-232-million-in-four-weeks).

#### Risks specific to projects that run only on Base (evidence behind each)
- Count share: Base fell to about 10% of transactions in the week to 2026-08-31 and Solana held 76% over four weeks to 2026-09-22 — [Coin-Turk](https://en.coin-turk.com/solana-overtakes-base-with-90yuzde-share-in-x402-transaction-volume-and-count/); [Solana Compass](https://solanacompass.com/news/solana-processes-76-of-all-x402-ai-agent-transactions-232-million-in-four-weeks).
- Buyer concentration and wash activity: about 4,000 wallets drive about 90% of adjusted volume (per the Visa executive's thread via The Defiant), and gamed transactions averaged 63.94% of the total in Nov 2025 to Feb 2026 — [The Defiant](https://thedefiant.io/news/infrastructure/visa-s-sheffield-pegs-adjusted-x402-volume-at-19m); [4pillars](https://research.4pillars.io/en/research/x402-on-polygon-real-or-gamed).
- Stripe steering: x402 on Base only versus MPP on Tempo and Solana, with Tempo described as Stripe's default chain for new features — [Stripe docs](https://docs.stripe.com/payments/machine); [Tempo](https://tempo.xyz).
- Portability: facilitators and SDKs already span many chains (Dexter eleven; CDP five; Faremeter EVM plus Solana; the reference repo has packages for nine chain families) — [Dexter](https://dexter.cash); [CDP network support](https://docs.cdp.coinbase.com/x402/network-support); [x402-foundation/x402](https://github.com/x402-foundation/x402).
- Foundation neutrality: x402 is no longer Coinbase's repo, and Base is not named as a Foundation member in the July article — [Solana Compass, 2026-07-15](https://solanacompass.com/news/linux-foundation-launches-x402-foundation-as-open-standards-body-for-ai-agent-payments).
- Session-style billing: MPP sessions are built in; x402's equivalent (`batch-settlement`) is EVM-only and relatively new — [x402-foundation/x402](https://github.com/x402-foundation/x402); [QuickNode](https://www.quicknode.com/blog/x402-vs-mpp).

### Inferences
- The likely end state is a 402 meta-layer: servers advertise several payment methods and clients pick one. In that world, the durable value sits in discovery (marketplaces), identity and spend controls, and billing, not in the choice of chain. Base-only projects that sell a chain-specific facilitator or a Base-only wallet are the most exposed.
- Facilitator pricing power is low. CDP's $0.001 per transaction after the free tier competes with free alternatives, and chain foundations subsidise their own. Micropayment sellers pricing at or below $0.001 per call will not cover a CDP fee outside batch settlement (my arithmetic from the cited price).
- Base-only projects face a narrative risk first (count leadership has moved) and a revenue risk later (Stripe steering, buyer concentration). The dollar-share evidence still favours Base as of April and per Coinbase's Q2 claim.
- Card networks are a medium-term competitor for consumer-sized purchases and an ally for machine micropayments. The thing to watch is whether AP4M's credential and settlement layer becomes the default for agents spending on behalf of businesses, which could route around any single x402 chain.

### Gaps
- No facilitator-share data after January 2026, so I cannot say whether Coinbase, Dexter, PayAI or chain-run facilitators gained during the window.
- Whether any Foundation member is paying fees, and how the Foundation's governance treats rival protocols, is not described in the sources I read.
- The sources do not say whether Base is a Foundation member in July (it was in the April intent list); this could be a tier-reporting gap, not a change.
- No independent evidence on how many Base-only x402 projects exist or how their revenue is trending.
