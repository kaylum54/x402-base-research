# Due diligence: FluxA, ATXP and Locus (agent payment providers that settle x402 volume on Base)

> Status: complete (written 2026-10-11): FluxA, ATXP and Locus profiles plus comparison table. The file was rewritten after each project.
> Scope and conventions: today is 2026-10-11; "last six months" = 2026-04-11 to 2026-10-11. No token price analysis. Every claim is dated; "(unconfirmed)" marks claims I could not verify; "Inference:" marks my reading of evidence; "Gap:" marks what I could not find. All web pages and APIs below were accessed on 2026-10-11 unless another date is given. x402scan figures come from its public tRPC endpoints (the ones the x402scan website itself uses), pulled 2026-10-11 between about 12:30 and 15:00 UTC.

## Answers to the key questions (takeaways)

### FluxA
- **Who runs it:** FluxA's own blog names Kevin Yang as "Co-founder and CEO" (post of 2026-09-21); no other team member is named by an official source; the legal entity is FLUXA LIMITED (site footer), terms governed by Singapore law; a 2025-09-18 BlockBeats piece says the founding team are former Alibaba and Ant Group executives (unnamed, unconfirmed). Funding: no evidence of outside funding found.
- **How much usage is real wallet activity:** very little of the x402scan volume looks like organic use. In the last 30 days, 94.2% of FluxA's transactions (129,300 of 137,267) are $0.001 payments to one recipient from about 1,300 wallets that pay on metronome-like timers (14 of 14 wallets I sampled pay only this recipient, 11 of them every 6,652 to 6,654 seconds and 3 every 6,178 seconds, and the 100 newest payments landed on only 37 distinct seconds), so they are machine-generated; 96.8% of the dollars ($312K of $322K) come from two wallets making 43 transactions, many of them round-number transfers ($10K to $70K) to a handful of addresses. What is left (about 8K transactions and about $10K) is at most a few percent of the headline numbers. Separately, x402scan double-counted FluxA payments for part of the window (payments routed through an escrow showed as two transfers), which inflates the "2.66M transactions" figure by an amount I estimate at roughly 0.45 to 0.6 million (up to 0.9 million if earlier months were also affected), so the six-month count of settled payments is closer to 2.1 to 2.2 million.

### ATXP
- **Who is behind it:** Circuit & Chisel, a New York-based company co-founded by two ex-Stripe crypto executives, Louis Amira (CEO, formerly Stripe's head of crypto and AI partnerships) and David Noël-Romas (CTO, formerly Stripe's head of crypto engineering); it raised a $19.2M seed announced 2025-09-22 (co-leads Primary Venture Partners and ParaFi Capital; Stripe, Coinbase Ventures, Solana Ventures, Samsung Next and Polygon Labs also invested). This is the best-funded and best-documented of the three.
- **Why Base activity stopped:** the cause is not stated by ATXP. What the record shows: all of the hub's x402 volume on x402scan sits inside 2026-04-07 to 2026-06-18 (Base) and 2026-04-09 to 2026-06-26 (Solana, plus one $0.01 transfer on 2026-07-28); the company's 2026-06-10 press release claimed the #1 spot on the "x402 leaderboard" using figures that match x402scan within about 1% and the flow ended days later; the SDK moved to "settle at session close / up-to" metering on 2026-06-17/18, the last SDK commit is 2026-06-30, the blog has had no post since 2026-04-28, and the company now leads with a different product (Unbiased / Pareto, an LLM, listed on OpenRouter on 2026-09-17). The Base hub address held about $8,500 in mid-June, was swept to about $47 by 2026-07-03 and has barely moved since.
- **Where it went:** nowhere I can see. The hub's live 402 response on 2026-10-11 offers only an MPP (Stripe/Tempo "Machine Payments Protocol") challenge, no x402 header, and the same Base address is not receiving meaningful payments. Solana stopped too. Inference: the x402 traffic was tied to a marketing and consumer-app push (April to June 2026) and the payments product was then deprioritised in favour of Pareto.
- **How much looks real:** low and unproven. In random samples of Base payers, 10 of 10 had only ever paid ATXP's hub (no other x402 seller that x402scan indexes), and 14 of 14 are plain wallets that never sent a transaction themselves (nonce 0), with 13 of 13 balance reads at $0.00 (one read failed): single-purpose wallets, not general x402 clients. Traffic is on/off by day, grew from 256 transfers to 9,318 in the 24 hours around a paid press release (2026-04-15/16) and stopped within days of the next one (2026-06-10). It may include genuine consumer use of ATXP's own apps (the company says humans, not agents, were most of its users in March 2026), but it is not evidence of third-party x402 commerce.

### Locus
- **Team and funding:** a Y Combinator Fall 2025 company (verified on YC's own page: active, San Francisco, team of 2, founded 2025), legal name Locus Technologies Inc.; founders Cole Dermott (CEO, spent a summer at Coinbase on B2B payments) and Eliot Lee (ex-Scale AI), plus founding engineer Wes Jorgensen. Funding: YC's standard $500K deal is the likely amount (CB Insights lists $500K as a convertible note with Y Combinator and Spot VC named); no other round found. So "reportedly YC" is confirmed.
- **Product direction:** away from x402. From a spend-controlled USDC wallet (Nov 2025) to an AI "cofounder" for online businesses (Locus Founder, 2026-06-15/16) to Locus Pro, a prepaid balance for thousands of paid APIs delivered by MCP and a coding-agent plugin (announced 2026-08-20; plugin shipped 6 releases between 2026-09-03 and 2026-09-22; pre-registered benchmark published 2026-10-03 to 10-06).
- **The volume:** its x402 wrappers around 52 Bazaar resources have handled 38,339 transfers and $105.52 in 68 days; 99.1% of the last 30 days' 35,577 transfers (35,250) were one wallet's $0.001 test burst on 2026-09-28 to 09-30. What remains is 327 transfers and $16.76 in 30 days from a few dozen wallets. x402 is a side channel for Locus, not its business.

## FluxA

### One-line
FluxA is a Singapore-law "agent payments" company (entity: FLUXA LIMITED) that sells an AI-agent wallet with spending mandates, single-use virtual cards, an agent marketplace, and runs an x402 facilitator on Base; in plain words: a way to give an AI agent a budget and let it pay for things.

### x402 role
Facilitator (x402scan id `fluxa`, Base USDC; also lists "credits" and XRP schemes in its docs), buyer/agent-client tooling (wallet CLI, MCP server and "skill" files that tell agents how to pay), and a seller marketplace (AgentMarket / "Monetize"). It also authored "Open Deferred Payments (ODP)", a deferred-settlement scheme for x402 v2.
- Facilitator endpoints and supported schemes: Base USDC at `https://facilitator.fluxapay.xyz/` (x402 v1 and v2, `eip155:8453`, covers gas via EIP-3009 `transferWithAuthorization`); "credits" (network `fluxa-monetize-credits`, an off-chain ledger) and XRP (`xrpl-mainnet`) at an endpoint on a `fluxapay-qa.xyz` domain (the docs do not say whether that is production). The docs claim FluxA is "the most active non-Coinbase x402 facilitator on Base by daily transaction count" (company claim; see traffic analysis below for what that count consists of). — [FluxA x402 docs](https://docs.fluxapay.xyz/x402/)
- FluxA's facilitator `/supported` endpoint on 2026-10-11 lists one signer for Base, `0x3be45f576696a2fD5a93C1330cD19F1607ab311D`, which is not among the six settlement addresses x402scan tracks for FluxA (nonce of that address on 2026-10-11: 12,232 transactions sent, so any untracked volume is small so far but growing). — [FluxA /supported](https://facilitator.fluxapay.xyz/supported); six tracked addresses from [x402scan facilitator list](https://www.x402scan.com/api/trpc/public.facilitators.list?input=%7B%22json%22%3A%7B%22timeframe%22%3A30%2C%22chain%22%3A%22base%22%2C%22sorting%22%3A%7B%22id%22%3A%22tx_count%22%2C%22desc%22%3Atrue%7D%2C%22pagination%22%3A%7B%22page%22%3A0%2C%22page_size%22%3A100%7D%7D%7D); nonce read via Base RPC (`eth_getTransactionCount`, https://mainnet.base.org) on 2026-10-11.
- Facilitator registered on x402scan through a pull request by FluxA's engineer (merged 2026-05-13); a second PR adding four "backup" signer addresses (2026-05-17) was closed without merging. — [x402scan PR 851](https://github.com/Merit-Systems/x402scan/pull/851), [PR 889](https://github.com/Merit-Systems/x402scan/pull/889)

### Pace verdict
Moderate and lumpy; not slow on product, thin on public code. Reference points: the x402 core repo ran 67 to 115 commits per month in the same months (Apr 67, May 115, Jun 82, Jul 81, Aug 109, Sep 102; [x402-foundation/x402 commits](https://github.com/x402-foundation/x402/commits/main)), and a small team using AI coding tools in 2026 would be expected to ship releases monthly or faster with weekly commits. FluxA's public CLI repo got 71 commits in six months but zero in May, one in August and a 71-day gap between npm releases; weeks with no commit are common. Against that, the company ships visible features (cards, markets, Token Plan, linked cards) in bursts that line up with partner events (Baidu 06-05, 09-20), two or more blog posts a quarter, and a team that already uses Claude Code and Codex. The backend is private, so the real pace is not observable. I would call the shipping pace "monthly-ish with two quiet spells" and the public code "thin".

### Green and red flags
**Green**
- Best-funded of the three ($19.2M seed, Stripe/Coinbase/Solana/Polygon on the cap table) with named, credible founders and engineers who attach their employer to their GitHub profiles.
- Real, working software: SDKs, CLI, hub endpoint, three consumer apps; the company's leaderboard figures match x402scan, so its numbers are not invented.
- Honest about uncertainty in the press: Amira said in March 2026 that nobody was placing large bets because "it's just so unclear".

**Red**
- The x402 spike lined up with paid press releases and stopped within weeks of the second; no meaningful x402 transfers since late June (Base: last 2026-06-18; Solana: last bulk 2026-06-26, one dust transfer 2026-07-28).
- Single-purpose payer wallets, on/off days, no independent third-party buyers visible.
- 165-post blog written in about ten weeks, then silence; SDK silent since 2026-06-30; CLI silent since April.
- New product (Pareto) does not mention ATXP; the old `iou.atxp.ai` $336K episode in Oct-Nov 2025 is unexplained and the host is dead.
- Mixed account numbers (500K users in April, over 1M accounts in June, versus 108.5K Base and 192.6K Solana payer wallets all-time) and a $5 versus 10-IOU discrepancy between docs and brand page: signs of loose metrics.

**How much of the volume looks like real use (evidence).** x402scan, Base plus Solana, 2026-04-07 to 2026-07-28: 881,817 transfers and $353,491 in total.
1. Platform-originated rather than market-driven: all 10 randomly sampled Base payers had only ever paid ATXP's hub (no other x402 seller that x402scan indexes), and 14 of 14 sampled payers are wallets that never sent a transaction (nonce 0); 13 of 13 balance reads were $0.00 on 2026-10-11 (one read failed). A sample of 1,400 Base rows (14 pages of 100 spread across the period) came from 1,013 distinct payers, 151 of whom appear more than once.
2. Fixed price points: of 1,400 sampled Base rows, $0.201 is 12.3%, $0.50 is 10.6%, $0.01 is 8.3%, $0.105 is 2.5%, the rest metered odd amounts (for example $0.001656): these look like ATXP's own tool and app prices (ATXP's email send is $0.01), i.e. users of ATXP's own apps, not agents shopping among sellers.
3. Timing: Base was active on only 40 of the 73 days between 2026-04-07 and 2026-06-18 (zero-transfer gaps of 2 to 8 days in a row, for example 2026-05-21 to 05-28), Solana on 63 of the 79 days between 2026-04-09 and 2026-06-26; traffic jumped from 256 transfers (2026-04-15) to 9,318 transfers from 4,673 wallets (2026-04-16), the day of the "tops x402 and MPP leaderboards" press release; the bulk stopped on 2026-06-11 (Base) and 2026-06-26 (Solana), one to two weeks after the 2026-06-10 release.
4. Fair counter-evidence: the company says (and Payments Dive reported on 2026-03-05) that most users were humans signing up through its apps; 30,000 songs in a few weeks is plausible at $0.50 each; the payer wallets may be per-account wallets of real people using Music/Pics/Chat.
Net: the on-chain count is best read as ATXP's own app traffic made visible through x402, possibly with real human users but not market adoption of x402. I cannot assign a reliable organic percentage; as an x402-commerce measure I would count it as close to zero, and as evidence of product use at most a minority of the 108.5K and 192.6K wallets. The brief's "108.5K Base buyers and 192.6K Solana buyers" are wallets, not customers.

### Verdict
**Likely abandoned (as an x402 / payments project; the company itself is alive and funded). Momentum: Stalled.**
Circuit & Chisel is a credible, well-funded team that used x402 and MPP leaderboards as marketing in April-June 2026, then let the payments product go quiet while it built and shipped an LLM (Pareto). The hub's Base address is idle, the SDK has not moved since 2026-06-30, and my best reading (inference, not confirmed by ATXP) is that the traffic was tied to ATXP's own launch campaign and apps rather than independent x402 demand, and stopped when that push ended; I found no sign that x402 settlement itself failed. I would not follow ATXP daily for x402; check monthly for a new SDK release or any movement of the hub's balance, and watch Pareto only if model access matters to the audience. It is a useful cautionary example: a top-of-the-leaderboard seller with 100K+ "buyers" whose x402 flow stopped within weeks of its #1 press release.

Locus (legal name Locus Technologies Inc., Y Combinator Fall 2025) began as a spend-controlled USDC wallet for AI agents and is now mostly a prepaid-credit service that gives coding agents one balance for thousands of paid APIs (Locus Pro), plus an AI "cofounder" that builds and runs an online business (Locus Founder). Its x402 presence is a set of 52 pay-per-call wrappers around third-party APIs that earn almost nothing.
Sources: [YC company page](https://www.ycombinator.com/companies/locus), [YC launch post](https://www.ycombinator.com/launches/Oj6-locus-payment-infrastructure-for-ai-agents), [Locus Founder about page](https://locusfounder.com/about), [plugin README](https://github.com/locus-technologies/locus-pro-plugin).

Four of the 52 Bazaar entries point at staging hosts (`x402.stage.paywithlocus.com`), so a test deployment is listed in production discovery. — [Bazaar discovery API](https://api.cdp.coinbase.com/platform/v2/x402/discovery/resources?limit=1000&offset=0) (read 2026-10-11, 32,207 listings), [x402scan Base 30-day origin list](https://www.x402scan.com/api/trpc/public.sellers.bazaar.list?input=%7B%22json%22%3A%7B%22timeframe%22%3A30%2C%22chain%22%3A%22base%22%2C%22sorting%22%3A%7B%22id%22%3A%22tx_count%22%2C%22desc%22%3Atrue%7D%2C%22pagination%22%3A%7B%22page%22%3A0%2C%22page_size%22%3A100%7D%7D%7D), [Locus docs index (snippet)](https://docs.paywithlocus.com/index.md)

### Pace verdict
Fast, but not on x402. A two-person founding team plus one engineer shipped a consumer product (June), a developer product (August), 6 plugin releases in 19 days (September, 68 commits that month against 109 and 102 on the x402 reference repo in August and September) and a 38-commit benchmark repo in four days (October). That clears the monthly-releases and weekly-commits bar for a small team using AI coding tools, but the x402 wrappers themselves have no public code or release history and were registered in a single day (2026-08-08). There are no commits in the plugin repo after 2026-09-22 (19 days), which is a short pause, not a stall.

### Direction
Stated: payment infrastructure for agents with budgets, permissions and audit trails (Nov 2025); "an AI that builds and runs your business" (Jun 2026); one balance for every paid API (Aug 2026 onward). The shipping record follows the stated direction closely and each step moved away from on-chain payments toward a credits ledger funded by card or USDC: the plugin README tells users to add credits in a dashboard and describes a Stripe funding handoff, and the benchmark compares Locus Pro with wiring up seven vendors directly. x402 and MPP are mentioned in the docs as one way to open or fund a workspace and as a distribution channel in the Bazaar, not as the product.

### Competitors
- API-credit gateways for agents: OpenRouter (the stated analogy), plus MCP and tool marketplaces; in x402: Merit Systems' AgentCash, Heurist, BlockRun and AIsa (all covered by other researchers; none profiled here).
- Agent wallets with spend controls: Coinbase agentic wallets, Crossmint, Skyfire, FluxA and ATXP (in this file). In dollars settled through x402 on Base Locus is far behind both (about $106 all-time against FluxA's $660K and ATXP's $122K on Base).
- AI "cofounder" products: general AI app and site builders (not researched).
Who is ahead: Coinbase on x402 plumbing; OpenRouter-type gateways on model access; on the narrow idea of a pre-funded API credit across thousands of endpoints Locus may be among the earliest, but I found no independent usage numbers.

### Green and red flags
**Green**
- Verified YC company with named founders whose X and LinkedIn links are on the YC pages, and a founding engineer who declares his role on GitHub.
- Ships constantly and publishes: 6 plugin releases in 19 days; a pre-registered benchmark with the conflict of interest stated, raw traces and both original and corrected results kept.
- Honest-looking small numbers: the wrappers' Bazaar calls (328 in 30 days) and the x402scan transfers (327 once the burst is removed) agree.

**Red**
- x402 volume is trivial ($105.52 in 68 days) and 99% of the last 30 days' transfers are one wallet's three-day burst at $0.001 each.
- No usage, revenue or customer figures anywhere; team of two to three; the pivots (wallet, AI cofounder, API credits) are fast.
- Four staging endpoints in the production Bazaar; wrappers created in one day with a generic description ("Locus public pay-per-use API").
- The company's own benchmark is a marketing asset by its own admission.
- Funding detail is thin and unconfirmed beyond the YC batch.
- Product pages could not be inspected from this environment (TLS error), so several product statements are second-hand.

**How much of the volume looks like real use (evidence).** x402scan Base, 2026-08-04 to 2026-10-11: 38,339 transfers and $105.52.
1. Test burst: 35,250 transfers ($40.32) on 2026-09-28, 09-29 and 09-30 (92% of all-time transfers; 99.1% of the last 30 days' transfers). In four sampled pages of 100 rows each from those days (400 rows), every row was from a single wallet (`0x27abcddd44c4959ac729d080703e677f13a3248c`) paying exactly $0.001, at up to about 16 payments a second (100 rows in 6 seconds). Verdict: load test or script; it cannot be organic demand. Whether Locus or someone else ran it is unknown.
2. Smaller scripts: on 2026-08-06 one wallet made 100 payments of $0.001 in 16 seconds; on 2026-09-02 another made 100 payments of $0.038 in 25 minutes. Those look like tests or demos.
3. What is left: 327 transfers and $16.76 in the last 30 days (62 transfers and $1.33 in the last 7 days), from a few dozen wallets (34 payers in 30 days, 79 all-time, at most 10 on any day), spread over many endpoints at 1 to 6 payers each, with odd metered amounts (for example $0.038001, $0.313) that look like real calls to Replicate, Browser Use, Hunter and similar. This is the only part that could be real use; it is developer experimentation at the level of a few dollars a month.
Net: roughly 1% of transfers and about 29% of dollars in the last 30 days look like real use, and the dollars are small enough ($16.76 in 30 days) that the percentage means little. Locus's x402 numbers say nothing about its business.

### Verdict
**Credible but small. Momentum: Accelerating (outside x402; x402 itself is negligible).**
Locus is a verified Y Combinator company with a named, public-facing founder pair and a founding engineer, shipping new products every two to three months and publishing unusually careful evidence for its latest one. It is not an x402 story: the wrappers earn about $17 a month once a single-wallet test burst is removed, and every product step has moved toward a prepaid credit balance rather than on-chain settlement. Expect more developer-agent distribution (plugins, skills, MCP listings, benchmarks) rather than on-chain volume; follow it for the agent-spend idea and the Locus Pro roll-out, not for x402 numbers. The main uncertainties are whether the credits business has real paying users and whether the pace holds with two to three people.
